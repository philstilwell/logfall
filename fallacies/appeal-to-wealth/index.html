Appeal to wealth

Occurs when a claim is treated as more credible or correct mainly because it comes from a rich, famous, or financially successful person.

Rationality danger

Appeal to wealth threatens rationality by creating argument derailment: occurs when a claim is treated as more credible or correct mainly because it comes from a rich, famous, or financially successful person. The module should make the user see how confidence, attention, or burden shifts without a matching increase in evidential support.

Primary distortion

argument derailment.

Category profile

It moves attention away from the claim's evidential status and toward a pressure tactic, distraction, or rhetorical maneuver.

Dynamics to notice

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Tactical frame. Shortcut: It moves attention away from the claim's evidential status and toward a pressure tactic, distraction, or rhetorical maneuver. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Warning signs

Warning signs: appeal to wealth often appears when a discussion uses tactical pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion. Also watch for aliases or nearby labels: argumentum ad crumenam.
