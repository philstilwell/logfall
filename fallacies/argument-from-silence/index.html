Argument from silence

A conclusion deemed true based on the lack of opposition.

Evidential

Definition

A conclusion deemed true based on the lack of opposition.

Illustrative example

Because my opponent failed to address my point, my point is validated.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

If a point is not addressed, rarely does it become anything other than an unaddressed point. However, this is context-dependent. If you see a vampire sitting across from you on the subway, and none of the other passengers seem interested, you might first consider what medication you might have taken that morning before wielding your wooden stake since the silence of the other passengers in such a setting would be unusual. The key question to ask is "How unusual is this silence?"

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

The disinterest and subsequent silence of doctors on the claims of dubious medial remedies is often spun to imply their tacit support, or at minimum, their lack of opposition.
The phrase "no comment", which can be uttered for a wide variety of reasons, is often spun by the media to evoke suspicion of a secret or mystery.
