Fallacy profile

Appeal to emotion

Occurs when a conclusion is pushed mainly by triggering fear, pity, outrage, pride, or hope rather than by showing that the conclusion follows from the evidence.

EpistemicTacticalEmotional

Definition

Occurs when a conclusion is pushed mainly by triggering fear, pity, outrage, pride, or hope rather than by showing that the conclusion follows from the evidence.

Illustrative example

If you vote against this bill, remember the faces of the children who will suffer, and ask yourself how you will sleep at night.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Emotion is not irrational by itself. The fallacy appears when emotion substitutes for the reasoning that still needs to be supplied.

Prompt 3

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

In 2024 campaign advertising, immigration, crime, and abortion messages often relied on fear-laden images and worst-case anecdotes whose emotional force far exceeded the actual argument being made.
The spread of AI-generated political ads and deepfakes in 2024 showed how cheaply campaigns can trigger outrage or panic before voters have time to inspect the evidence.
