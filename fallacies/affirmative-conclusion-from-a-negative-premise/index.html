Fallacy profile

Affirmative conclusion from a negative premise

Occurs when a syllogism tries to draw a positive conclusion even though one of the premises is negative in a way that cannot support that conclusion.

Formal

Definition

Occurs when a syllogism tries to draw a positive conclusion even though one of the premises is negative in a way that cannot support that conclusion.

Illustrative example

This source is not anonymous. Anonymous sources are unreliable. Therefore this source is reliable.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Ruling something out does not automatically place it into the positive category you want. 'Not bad' does not by itself mean 'good,' and 'not that' does not by itself mean 'this.'

Rationality danger

Affirmative conclusion from a negative premise threatens rationality by creating invalid inference wearing the costume of necessity: occurs when a syllogism tries to draw a positive conclusion even though one of the premises is negative in a way that cannot support that conclusion. The module should make the user see how confidence, attention, or burden shifts without a matching increase in evidential support.

Primary distortion

invalid inference wearing the costume of necessity.

Category profile

It gives a conclusion the feel of deductive force even when the structure does not license it.

Dynamics to notice

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Formal frame. Shortcut: It gives a conclusion the feel of deductive force even when the structure does not license it. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Warning signs

Warning signs: affirmative conclusion from a negative premise often appears when a discussion uses formal pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.

Mechanic

Premise toggler / counterexample machine: use the row's example as the initial scenario, then force the user to separate evidence, inference, emotion, wording, and missing alternatives.

User action

Toggle premises as true or false, then try to make the conclusion false while the premises stay true.

Feedback logic

If a possible world keeps the premises true while the conclusion fails, the app highlights the invalid step.

Confidence gap check

Compare how persuasive the current example feels with how much support it actually earns.

Case studies

Documented cases chosen to stress-test the pattern with named sources rather than loose hypotheticals.

Tribal arguments often move from 'we are not them' and 'they are bad' to 'therefore we are trustworthy,' even though the conclusion has not been earned.

Media and politics frequently treat the failure of one rival source as if it directly validated a different source that still needs its own support.
