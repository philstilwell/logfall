Association fallacy

Occurs when a claim is accepted or dismissed because of some irrelevant association rather than because of the merits of the claim itself.

Rationality danger

Association fallacy threatens rationality by creating argument derailment: occurs when a claim is accepted or dismissed because of some irrelevant association rather than because of the merits of the claim itself. The module should make the user see how confidence, attention, or burden shifts without a matching increase in evidential support.

Primary distortion

argument derailment.

Category profile

It moves attention away from the claim's evidential status and toward a pressure tactic, distraction, or rhetorical maneuver.

Dynamics to notice

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Tactical frame with secondary pressure from Evidential. Shortcut: It moves attention away from the claim's evidential status and toward a pressure tactic, distraction, or rhetorical maneuver. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Warning signs

Warning signs: association fallacy often appears when a discussion uses tactical pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion. Also watch for aliases or nearby labels: guilt by association.

Political arguments routinely assume that if a bad person, rival group, or disliked movement touched an idea, the idea itself must be false or tainted.
