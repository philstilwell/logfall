Fallacy profile

Proof by verbosity

Submitting an argument too complex, obtuse or verbose to reasonably deal with.

LinguisticTactical

Definition

Submitting an argument too complex, obtuse or verbose to reasonably deal with.

Illustrative example

Insomuch as it remains within our agency, it behooves us to eschew obfuscation, and espouse elucidation.

Reference

Legacy number

64

Family

Informal Fallacy

Aliases

argumentum verbosium, proof by intimidation

Quick check

Has the wording shifted, blurred, or changed meaning mid-argument?

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

This is often a tactic used in formal speeches. If not all the many minor points introduced are not addressed by the opponent, the verbose contender will claim victory.

Questions to ask

Use these category-based prompts to audit similar arguments.

Prompt 1

Has the wording shifted, blurred, or changed meaning mid-argument?

Prompt 2

Is the argument still addressing the original issue, or has the conversation been steered away?

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

Donald Rumsfeld once said...There are known knowns. These are things we know that we know. There are known unknowns. That is to say, there are things that we know we don't know. But there are also unknown unknowns. There are things we don't know we don't know.
