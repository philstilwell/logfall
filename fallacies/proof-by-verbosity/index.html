Proof by verbosity

Occurs when a claim is protected by an avalanche of words, side points, jargon, or branching assertions that overwhelm reasonable scrutiny and create the illusion of depth.

Definition

Why this kind of mistake matters

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Tactical frame with secondary pressure from Linguistic. Shortcut: It moves attention away from the claim's evidential status and toward a pressure tactic, distraction, or rhetorical maneuver. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: proof by verbosity often appears when a discussion uses tactical pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion. Also watch for aliases or nearby labels: argumentum verbosium, proof by intimidation.
