Gambler's fallacy

Occurs when someone thinks past outcomes of independent events make a future independent outcome more or less likely than it really is.

MathematicalCausal

Definition

Occurs when someone thinks past outcomes of independent events make a future independent outcome more or less likely than it really is.

Illustrative example

The coin has landed heads five times in a row, so tails is due next.

Reference

Legacy number

56

Family

Informal Fallacy

Aliases

monte carlo fallacy, hot hand fallacy

Quick check

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Random sequences naturally contain streaks. When the events are truly independent, the next event does not remember the last one.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

People sometimes feel that after many routine flights or medical screenings in a row, a disaster or diagnosis is somehow 'due,' even though independent risks do not balance themselves that way.
In gambling and investing language, talk of a roulette table or slot machine being 'ready to pay out' often confuses a streaky pattern with a real shift in odds.
