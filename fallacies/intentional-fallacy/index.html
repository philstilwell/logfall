Intentional fallacy

Occurs when the creator's intended meaning is treated as irrelevant in contexts where that intention is actually important to understanding the work or statement.

PerspectivalLinguistic

Definition

Occurs when the creator's intended meaning is treated as irrelevant in contexts where that intention is actually important to understanding the work or statement.

Illustrative example

The author says the essay was satirical, but we should ignore that completely and read it only through our own current assumptions.

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Perspectival frame with secondary pressure from Linguistic. Shortcut: It mistakes one standpoint, timeframe, or interpretive frame for a complete evidential view. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: intentional fallacy often appears when a discussion uses perspectival pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.
