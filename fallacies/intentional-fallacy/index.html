Intentional fallacy

The assumption that the meaning intended by an author of a concept is not relevant to the understanding of that concept.

LinguisticPerspectival

Definition

The assumption that the meaning intended by an author of a concept is not relevant to the understanding of that concept.

Illustrative example

We ought to interpret "right to bear arms" within the context of our own time, and ignore the intent of the authors of the phrase.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Sometimes the evidence for the author's intentions is not in the actual literature, but must be extracted from other sources such as personal letters and notes.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

Some claim that Einstein was a theist based on some of his statements referring to "God". A more careful examination of his personal correspondence seems to suggest that he was, at most, a deist.
Some might suggest that Mark Twain's use of the "n" word belies the author's racism, not taking into consideration the more benign use of the term in that era, nor any literary device Twain might have been employing.
