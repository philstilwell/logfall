Intentional fallacy

Occurs when the creator's intended meaning is treated as irrelevant in contexts where that intention is actually important to understanding the work or statement.

PerspectivalLinguistic

Definition

Occurs when the creator's intended meaning is treated as irrelevant in contexts where that intention is actually important to understanding the work or statement.

Illustrative example

The author says the essay was satirical, but we should ignore that completely and read it only through our own current assumptions.

Why it matters

Why this mistake matters

Intentional fallacy threatens rationality by creating a narrow vantage point pretending to be the whole terrain: occurs when the creator's intended meaning is treated as irrelevant in contexts where that intention is actually important to understanding the work or statement. The module should make the user see how confidence, attention, or burden shifts without a matching increase in evidential support.

Main reasoning problem

a narrow vantage point pretending to be the whole terrain.

Why this kind of mistake matters

It mistakes one standpoint, timeframe, or interpretive frame for a complete evidential view.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Perspectival frame with secondary pressure from Linguistic. Shortcut: It mistakes one standpoint, timeframe, or interpretive frame for a complete evidential view. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: intentional fallacy often appears when a discussion uses perspectival pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.

How to fix it

How to fix it

Case studies

Documented cases chosen to stress-test the pattern with named sources rather than loose hypotheticals.

Debates over constitutional language, literature, and political slogans often swing too far toward present-day interpretation while discarding what the author or framers were trying to do.

Online arguments about memes, jokes, and clips frequently ignore context and stated intent altogether, then treat the most inflammatory possible reading as the only serious one.
