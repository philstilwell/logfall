Broken window fallacy

Occurs when destruction or forced replacement is treated as an economic gain because the visible spending is counted while the unseen losses and forgone alternatives are ignored.

EvidentialMathematical

Definition

Occurs when destruction or forced replacement is treated as an economic gain because the visible spending is counted while the unseen losses and forgone alternatives are ignored.

Illustrative example

The storm wrecked half the boardwalk, but at least all the rebuilding will be great for the local economy.

Why this kind of mistake matters

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Evidential frame with secondary pressure from Mathematical. Shortcut: It overstates, understates, cherry-picks, or misallocates the force of evidence. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: broken window fallacy often appears when a discussion uses evidential pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.
