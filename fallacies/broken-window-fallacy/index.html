Broken window fallacy

An argument which disregards lost opportunity costs (typically non-obvious, difficult to determine or otherwise hidden) associated with destroying property of others, or other ways of externalizing costs onto others.

MathematicalEvidential

Definition

An argument which disregards lost opportunity costs (typically non-obvious, difficult to determine or otherwise hidden) associated with destroying property of others, or other ways of externalizing costs onto others.

Illustrative example

After downtown vandalism, a mayor praises the damage for 'creating jobs' for glaziers while ignoring the shops, wages, and purchases that would have existed if the windows had never been broken.
