Linearity fallacy

Occurs when someone assumes that doubling the input will double the output even though the system has thresholds, saturation, feedback loops, or diminishing returns.

ConceptualMathematical

Definition

Occurs when someone assumes that doubling the input will double the output even though the system has thresholds, saturation, feedback loops, or diminishing returns.

Illustrative example

One dose gave a modest result, so taking twice as much should produce twice the benefit.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Many real systems change slowly at first, then rapidly, then plateau, or even reverse after a threshold.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

Growth forecasts for social platforms often assume audience reach will keep scaling smoothly even after attention markets saturate.
Policy debates about climate, housing, and healthcare frequently ignore the non-linear effects of bottlenecks, incentives, and behavioral feedback.
