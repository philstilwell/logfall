Appeal to nature

An argument in which something is deemed correct or good if it is natural, and is deemed incorrect or bad if it is unnatural.

ConceptualPerspectival

Definition

An argument in which something is deemed correct or good if it is natural, and is deemed incorrect or bad if it is unnatural.

Illustrative example

Birds are monogamous. Therefore, humans should also be monogamous.

Why it misleads

A longer note from the source material, cleaned for readability.

It does not take much exploration of nature to realize that it is not a very coherent or desirable standard for human conduct. Nor is nature, as is, very amenable to human survival. This is why medicine perennially combats the influenza virus, and would eradicate it if possible. Interestingly, the most "natural" of all actions inherent to life is the striving for survival. This puts humans at odds with the rest of nature and what is "natural". This is why we ingest neither natural hemlock nor synthetic poisons.<br /><br />In many cases, once the act that is deemed "unnatural" is scrutinized, it is discovered that the act is merely emotionally unpalatable to the person making the categorization.

Case studies

Examples and discussion prompts attached to the original source entry.

Some claim that genetically modified crops are "wrong" for the sole reason that they are not what nature has naturally produced. There may be good reason for caution in growing genetically-modified crops, but the fact that they are merely "unnatural" is not good reason.
While there are sects that eschew medical intervention, most humans seek medical attention when they are injured rather than allowing nature to run its course.
Cocaine was once touted as a "natural" remedy for many ailments. Today, not many consider the use of cocaine to be a very wise treatment due to its addictive effects.
