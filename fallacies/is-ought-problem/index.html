Is-ought problem

Occurs when a descriptive claim about what is common, natural, or actual is treated as if it directly established what ought to be done.

Definition

Occurs when a descriptive claim about what is common, natural, or actual is treated as if it directly established what ought to be done.

Why it matters

Why this mistake matters

Is-ought problem threatens rationality by creating bad category architecture: occurs when a descriptive claim about what is common, natural, or actual is treated as if it directly established what ought to be done. The module should make the user see how confidence, attention, or burden shifts without a matching increase in evidential support.

Main reasoning problem

bad category architecture.

Why this kind of mistake matters

It warps the conceptual map so that distinctions, boundaries, or levels of analysis mislead the inference.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Conceptual frame. Shortcut: It warps the conceptual map so that distinctions, boundaries, or levels of analysis mislead the inference. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: is-ought problem often appears when a discussion uses conceptual pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion. Also watch for aliases or nearby labels: isolated ought.

How to fix it

How to fix it

An ought needs a normative premise about goals, duties, or values. Facts alone do not generate that extra step.

Case studies

Documented cases chosen to stress-test the pattern with named sources rather than loose hypotheticals.

Arguments about gender, family, and competition often slide from claims about what is common in nature to claims about what is therefore morally required.

Workplace norms are regularly defended by saying they are simply how the market works, even when the moral question is precisely whether the market should work that way.
