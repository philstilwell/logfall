Denying a remote hypothetical

Occurs when a worthy hypothetical is rejected on grounds it is rare or improbable.

ConceptualEvidential

Definition

Occurs when a worthy hypothetical is rejected on grounds it is rare or improbable.

Illustrative example

Your scenario of having to choose between killing your child or your father or letting them both starve does is not applicable to my moral code since such a dilemma has probably never actually occurred in real life.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

If someone suggests they are in possession of a universal moral law, then even the most improbable scenarios is fair game to test that universal moral law. If you say killing another human is always wrong, then you'll need to address any conceivable scenario in which one human is faced with the choice of killing another.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

One remote scenario and moral dilemma is a situation in which you have the opportunity to save several lives on a trail by pushing a single person onto the tracks, thus killing him. The fact that the scenario is improbable does not mean that it cannot be used to test the validity and consistency of a moral principle.
Another scenario and moral dilemma is a situation in which there are 10 persons of various ages, sizes, intelligences, talents and genders on a lifeboat with only enough food for 20 meals. How do you allot the food? This remote scenario is a legitimate test of a moral code.
Another remote scenario is a situation in which a mother, hiding with her child and 20 other innocent people from men with guns, must decide to smother her crying child in order to save herself and the 20 others.
