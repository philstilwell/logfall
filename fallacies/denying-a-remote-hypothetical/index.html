Denying a remote hypothetical

Occurs when a hypothetical test case is dismissed as irrelevant merely because it is rare, extreme, or unlikely, even though the principle under debate is supposed to be universal.

Why this mistake matters

Main reasoning problem

Why this kind of mistake matters

It overstates, understates, cherry-picks, or misallocates the force of evidence.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Evidential frame with secondary pressure from Conceptual. Shortcut: It overstates, understates, cherry-picks, or misallocates the force of evidence. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: denying a remote hypothetical often appears when a discussion uses evidential pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.
