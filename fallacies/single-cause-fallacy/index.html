Single cause fallacy

Occurs when a complex outcome is explained as if one cause alone did the work, while other relevant causes are ignored or illegitimately minimized.

Definition

Occurs when a complex outcome is explained as if one cause alone did the work, while other relevant causes are ignored or illegitimately minimized.

Why it matters

Why this mistake matters

Main reasoning problem

A complex outcome is explained as if one cause alone did the work, while other relevant causes are ignored or illegitimately minimized.

Why this kind of mistake matters

It makes one causal pathway feel established before alternatives, confounders, and directionality are tested.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Causal frame with secondary pressure from Conceptual. Shortcut: It makes one causal pathway feel established before alternatives, confounders, and directionality are tested. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: single cause fallacy often appears when a discussion uses causal pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion. Also watch for aliases or nearby labels: joint effect, causal oversimplification.
