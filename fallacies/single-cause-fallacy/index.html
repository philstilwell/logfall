Single cause fallacy

Occurs when it is assumed that there is merely one cause of a phenomenon while other possibly contributing causes go undetected, are ignored or are illegitimately minimized.

CausalConceptual

Definition

Occurs when it is assumed that there is merely one cause of a phenomenon while other possibly contributing causes go undetected, are ignored or are illegitimately minimized.

Illustrative example

The drop in crime in our neighborhood is due to better policing.

Reference

Legacy number

52

Family

Informal Fallacy

Aliases

joint effect, causal oversimplification

Quick check

What evidence actually rules out coincidence, reverse causation, or a third factor?

Why it misleads

A longer note from the source material, cleaned for readability.

The actual cause of a local drop in crime may actually be a collection of causes such as the installation of surveillance cameras, a better local economy that has given would-be criminals jobs and money, improved forensics, new home entertainment hardware and software that keep would-be criminals preoccupied, better role models, and tougher laws that have locked up the most frequent offenders. This fallacy is a type of <a href="http://logfall.wordpress.com/false-dilemma/">false dilemma</a>.

Questions to ask

Prompt 2

Are the categories being used carefully, or are unlike things being treated as alike?

Case studies

Examples and discussion prompts attached to the original source entry.

Nearly every country that has become prosperous throughout history has had some of its citizens claiming that their greatness was entirely due to the superiority of their nationality or their particular god.
Whenever there is a school shooting, there emerge dozens of speculative opinions, each fingering a single cause such as poor parenting, violent video games, secular education, or the availability of guns. A responsible opinion would attempt cite evidence for the weight of each possible cause.
