Human standard fallacy

Occurs when a human classification, rule, or label is treated as if it automatically determined the underlying fact or moral status.

ConceptualPerspectival

Definition

Occurs when a human classification, rule, or label is treated as if it automatically determined the underlying fact or moral status.

Illustrative example

The platform's policy now defines this claim as misinformation, so it became false the moment the label was applied.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Institutional labels can matter legally or practically, but they do not by themselves create truth, value, or natural kinds.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

Markets and media often confuse approved, certified, or official with true, safe, or just.
Political rhetoric often treats whatever a legislature recognizes or forbids as automatically morally right or wrong.
