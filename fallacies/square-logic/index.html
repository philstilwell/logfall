Square logic

Occurs when an argument becomes so internally tangled that its pieces no longer form a coherent chain from premise to conclusion even though it sounds intricate.

Formal

Definition

Occurs when an argument becomes so internally tangled that its pieces no longer form a coherent chain from premise to conclusion even though it sounds intricate.

Illustrative example

If the story were false, fact-checkers would deny it. Because they denied it, they proved it was true, since they only deny dangerous truths.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Formal frame. Shortcut: It gives a conclusion the feel of deductive force even when the structure does not license it. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: square logic often appears when a discussion uses formal pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.
