All or nothing fallacy

Occurs when support for part of a view, or problems with part of a view, are treated as if they force total acceptance or total rejection of the whole package.

EpistemicEvidential

Definition

Occurs when support for part of a view, or problems with part of a view, are treated as if they force total acceptance or total rejection of the whole package.

Illustrative example

One error in this report means the entire investigation is worthless.

Unspecified

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Many systems of belief, institutions, and documents are mixed. Partial support or partial failure rarely justifies an everything-or-nothing verdict.

Case studies

In 2024 media battles, a single correction or mistaken detail was often used to dismiss an outlet's entire reporting on elections, immigration, or public health.

Religious, ideological, and policy debates often act as though rejecting one doctrine, statistic, or mechanism commits you to rejecting the entire worldview.
