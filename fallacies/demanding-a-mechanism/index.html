Fallacy profile

Demanding a mechanism

Occurs when strong evidence for a phenomenon is rejected solely because the underlying mechanism is still incomplete, disputed, or not yet fully understood.

Evidential

Definition

Occurs when strong evidence for a phenomenon is rejected solely because the underlying mechanism is still incomplete, disputed, or not yet fully understood.

Illustrative example

Until scientists can explain exactly how this pattern arises, we should not accept that the pattern is real.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Mechanisms matter, but evidence can justify belief before the full causal story is finished. Observation often outruns explanation.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

Public disputes about climate, evolution, and health regularly treat unresolved details in the mechanism as if they erased the larger evidential picture.
Research on AI transcription errors in 2024 showed that systems were inventing statements people never said; even before every technical cause was nailed down, the pattern itself was already evidence of a real reliability problem.
