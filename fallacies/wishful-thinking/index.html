Wishful thinking

In which a decision is encouraged based on what might be pleasing to imagine, rather than based on evidence or reason.

EvidentialEpistemicEmotional

Definition

In which a decision is encouraged based on what might be pleasing to imagine, rather than based on evidence or reason.

Illustrative example

If there were no life after death, then this life would be rather meaningless, and so I choose to believe we have an eternal soul.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

This fallacy is probably the most human of all fallacies. It is almost to suggest, that if you want or believe something strongly enough, that something must be or become true.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

If it were argued that the desire of the majority of citizens for universal health care coverage makes it imperative that we pass a universal health care coverage bill, this would be committing the wishful thinking fallacy. There may be no objective way for some countries to pay for the plan given their resources. The fact that something would make us happy does not mean we deserve or can realize that something.
