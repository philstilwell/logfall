Conjunction fallacy

Occurs when a more detailed scenario is treated as more probable than a less detailed scenario that already contains it.

ConceptualMathematical

Definition

Occurs when a more detailed scenario is treated as more probable than a less detailed scenario that already contains it.

Illustrative example

Because the candidate is young and progressive, it is more likely that she is both a climate activist and a vegan than that she is simply a climate activist.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: conjunction fallacy often appears when a discussion uses conceptual pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.

Case studies

Documented cases chosen to stress-test the pattern with named sources rather than loose hypotheticals.

Political profiling often mistakes a vivid stereotype for probability, assuming that a person matching one trait is more likely to match an even narrower bundle of traits as well.

AI narratives sometimes suggest that because a model appears articulate, it is more likely to be both highly capable and aligned than merely capable, even though the conjunction is narrower.

Appeal to probability

Occurs when someone assumes that because something could happen, it is therefore likely or inevitable that it will happen.

Base rate fallacy

Occurs when someone judges how likely a case is by focusing on vivid case-specific evidence while ignoring the underlying frequency of the thing in question.

Occurs when one side of a genuine contrast is denied or redefined so the opposing term has no place to apply.

Fallacy of necessity

Occurs when a condition that is necessary given someone's current description is treated as if it were permanently or universally necessary in the real world.
