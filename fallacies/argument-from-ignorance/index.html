Argument from ignorance

Definition

Illustrative example

Why it misleads

A longer note from the source material, cleaned for readability.

Phrases that often accompany this fallacy include "What are the odds!" and "Explain that!" The inability of the (or a particular) human brain to grasp the stochastic or conceptual complexities of a particular concept do not warrant the introduction of magic or a god or luck as an explanation.<hr />Note that there is warrant to dismiss out-of-hand claims that are logically or physically impossible. If I claim I possess a square triangle, or a flying reindeer, you are warranted in stating unequivocally that I am wrong.

Case studies

Examples and discussion prompts attached to the original source entry.

Some say we are warranted in believing that the Loch Ness Monster exists since there is no evidence to the contrary.
Some who do not accept a particular scientific theory often point to a single node in the matrix of converging lines of evidence for the theory, point out its short-comings, then claim the theory is wrong. If you've lived next to Betty and Bob for 20 years who are presumably married, and happen to see Betty with an unidentified man one afternoon, you are not warranted in believing that Betty and Bob are not married.
