Fallacy profile

Negative proof fallacy

Assuming that, because a premise cannot be proven false, the premise must be true; or that, because a premise cannot be proven true, the premise must be false.

Formal

Definition

Assuming that, because a premise cannot be proven false, the premise must be true; or that, because a premise cannot be proven true, the premise must be false.

Illustrative example

Can you prove I'm not wearing an invisible hat? If you can't, I'm justified in believing I am.

Quick check

Why it misleads

A longer note from the source material, cleaned for readability.

The degree of warrant for a given proposition is the degree of evidence that favors that proposition, not the absence of evidence that would falsify the proposition. The ability to say "You can't prove I'm not right" does not mean you are warranted in believing you are right.

Case studies

Examples and discussion prompts attached to the original source entry.

If someone claims that global warming is due to human activity, then justifies their position by saying "You can't prove human activity is not the cause of global warming", they have committed this fallacy.
In the U.S., until someone accused of a crime has been found guilty based on sufficient evidence, they are presumed innocent. This does not mean they <strong>are</strong> innocent of the crime. It is just that we start with the assumption that they are innocent until (if ever) sufficient evidence warrants our deeming them guilty.
