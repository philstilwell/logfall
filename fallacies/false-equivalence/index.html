False equivalence

Occurs when two things are treated as equivalent in seriousness, meaning, or explanatory weight despite relevant differences that make the comparison misleading.

Definition

Occurs when two things are treated as equivalent in seriousness, meaning, or explanatory weight despite relevant differences that make the comparison misleading.

Illustrative example

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Why this kind of mistake matters

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Conceptual frame with secondary pressure from Evidential. Shortcut: It warps the conceptual map so that distinctions, boundaries, or levels of analysis mislead the inference. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: false equivalence often appears when a discussion uses conceptual pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion. Also watch for aliases or nearby labels: moral equivalence.
