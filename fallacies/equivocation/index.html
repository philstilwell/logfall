Fallacy profile

Equivocation

Occurs when a key word or phrase slides between different meanings inside the same argument, creating the illusion of support.

Linguistic

Definition

Occurs when a key word or phrase slides between different meanings inside the same argument, creating the illusion of support.

Illustrative example

The AI model can 'learn,' and students learn, so the model must understand lessons in the same way students do.

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Equivocation often works because the same word is doing two jobs at once. The surface grammar stays stable while the meaning shifts underneath.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

Debates over content moderation regularly equivocate between censorship by the state and moderation decisions by private platforms, as if both were the same kind of speech restriction.
Public discussion of AI often slides between thin technical meanings of words such as 'reasoning,' 'understanding,' or 'hallucination' and thicker human meanings, which makes claims sound stronger than the evidence warrants.
