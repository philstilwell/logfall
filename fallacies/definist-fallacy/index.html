Definist fallacy

Occurs when a substantive question is illegitimately 'solved' by defining one contested concept into another.

LinguisticConceptual

Definition

Occurs when a substantive question is illegitimately 'solved' by defining one contested concept into another.

Illustrative example

Justice just means whatever the law permits, so once a policy is legal it is automatically just.

Why it matters

Why this mistake matters

Definist fallacy threatens rationality by creating word movement disguised as argument movement: occurs when a substantive question is illegitimately 'solved' by defining one contested concept into another. The module should make the user see how confidence, attention, or burden shifts without a matching increase in evidential support.

Main reasoning problem

word movement disguised as argument movement.

Why this kind of mistake matters

It lets ambiguity, framing, or unstable wording do work that evidence or valid inference should do.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Linguistic frame with secondary pressure from Conceptual. Shortcut: It lets ambiguity, framing, or unstable wording do work that evidence or valid inference should do. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: definist fallacy often appears when a discussion uses linguistic pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.

How to fix it

How to fix it
