Redeeming illogic with evidence

Occurs when someone demands empirical evidence before rejecting a concept that is already incoherent, self-contradictory, or logically impossible on its own terms.

Definition

Occurs when someone demands empirical evidence before rejecting a concept that is already incoherent, self-contradictory, or logically impossible on its own terms.

Illustrative example

Why it matters

Why this mistake matters

Redeeming illogic with evidence threatens rationality because someone demands empirical evidence before rejecting a concept that is already incoherent, self-contradictory, or logically impossible on its own terms.

Main reasoning problem

Someone demands empirical evidence before rejecting a concept that is already incoherent, self-contradictory, or logically impossible on its own terms.

Why this kind of mistake matters

It overstates, understates, cherry-picks, or misallocates the force of evidence.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Evidential frame. Shortcut: It overstates, understates, cherry-picks, or misallocates the force of evidence. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: redeeming illogic with evidence often appears when a discussion uses evidential pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.

How to fix it

How to fix it

Evidence matters for empirical claims, but it cannot rescue a contradiction. If the concept itself does not make sense, more observations do not repair it.
