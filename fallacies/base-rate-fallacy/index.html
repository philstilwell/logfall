Base rate fallacy

Occurs when someone judges how likely a case is by focusing on vivid case-specific evidence while ignoring the underlying frequency of the thing in question.

Mathematical

Definition

Occurs when someone judges how likely a case is by focusing on vivid case-specific evidence while ignoring the underlying frequency of the thing in question.

Illustrative example

This fraud filter is 98% accurate and flagged my transaction, so there is almost no chance the alert is wrong.

Why it matters

Why this mistake matters

Base rate fallacy threatens rationality by creating number-shaped confidence without proper numerical grounding: occurs when someone judges how likely a case is by focusing on vivid case-specific evidence while ignoring the underlying frequency of the thing in question. The module should make the user see how confidence, attention, or burden shifts without a matching increase in evidential support.

Main reasoning problem

number-shaped confidence without proper numerical grounding.

Why this kind of mistake matters

It makes quantities, probabilities, rates, or samples push confidence farther than the math permits.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Mathematical frame. Shortcut: It makes quantities, probabilities, rates, or samples push confidence farther than the math permits. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: base rate fallacy often appears when a discussion uses mathematical pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.

How to fix it

How to fix it

For rare conditions or rare events, even very accurate tests can generate more false positives than true positives. The missing question is not just 'How accurate is the test?' but 'How common is the condition before the test is applied?'
