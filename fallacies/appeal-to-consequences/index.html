Why it matters

Why this mistake matters

Appeal to consequences threatens rationality because someone treats the desirability or undesirability of a conclusion as if it were evidence that the conclusion is true or false.

Main reasoning problem

Someone treats the desirability or undesirability of a conclusion as if it were evidence that the conclusion is true or false.

Why this kind of mistake matters

It overstates, understates, cherry-picks, or misallocates the force of evidence.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Evidential frame with secondary pressure from Emotional. Shortcut: It overstates, understates, cherry-picks, or misallocates the force of evidence. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: appeal to consequences often appears when a discussion uses evidential pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.

How to fix it

How to fix it

Consequences matter for what we should do, but they do not by themselves determine what is true. A painful implication does not refute a claim, and a comforting implication does not confirm one.
