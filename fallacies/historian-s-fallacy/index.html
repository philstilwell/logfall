Historian's fallacy

Occurs when people in the past are judged as if they had the same information, background assumptions, and hindsight available to later observers.

Perspectival

Definition

Occurs when people in the past are judged as if they had the same information, background assumptions, and hindsight available to later observers.

Illustrative example

Anyone living in 2007 should have seen the financial crash coming exactly as we see it now.

Main reasoning problem

Why this kind of mistake matters

It mistakes one standpoint, timeframe, or interpretive frame for a complete evidential view.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Perspectival frame. Shortcut: It mistakes one standpoint, timeframe, or interpretive frame for a complete evidential view. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: historian's fallacy often appears when a discussion uses perspectival pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.
