Fallacy profile

If-by-whiskey

Occurs when someone uses strategically shifting language that seems to support both sides by quietly changing the meaning of the key term to suit the audience.

Tactical

Definition

Occurs when someone uses strategically shifting language that seems to support both sides by quietly changing the meaning of the key term to suit the audience.

Illustrative example

If by 'AI regulation' you mean stopping abuse, I support it. But if by 'AI regulation' you mean slowing innovation, I oppose it.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Tactical frame. Shortcut: It moves attention away from the claim's evidential status and toward a pressure tactic, distraction, or rhetorical maneuver. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: if-by-whiskey often appears when a discussion uses tactical pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.
