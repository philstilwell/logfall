Tu quoque

Occurs when criticism is answered not by engaging the issue, but by pointing to similar hypocrisy or wrongdoing elsewhere.

Definition

Occurs when criticism is answered not by engaging the issue, but by pointing to similar hypocrisy or wrongdoing elsewhere.

Illustrative example

Why it matters

Why this mistake matters

Tu quoque threatens rationality because criticism is answered not by engaging the issue, but by pointing to similar hypocrisy or wrongdoing elsewhere.

Main reasoning problem

Criticism is answered not by engaging the issue, but by pointing to similar hypocrisy or wrongdoing elsewhere.

Why this kind of mistake matters

It moves attention away from the claim's evidential status and toward a pressure tactic, distraction, or rhetorical maneuver.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Tactical frame with secondary pressure from Evidential. Shortcut: It moves attention away from the claim's evidential status and toward a pressure tactic, distraction, or rhetorical maneuver. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: tu quoque often appears when a discussion uses tactical pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion. Also watch for aliases or nearby labels: appeal to hypocrisy, look who's talking.
