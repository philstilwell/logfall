Equivocation fallacy

Occurs when a broad or harmless sense of a word is used to insinuate a narrower, stronger, or more loaded sense of the same word.

What to watch for

What to watch for

Watch the trigger, the shortcut, and the payoff. Trigger: the claim enters under the Linguistic frame. Shortcut: It lets ambiguity, framing, or unstable wording do work that evidence or valid inference should do. Payoff: the conclusion becomes easier to accept, reject, or ignore before the relevant comparison has been paid for.

Common warning signs

Warning signs: equivocation fallacy often appears when a discussion uses linguistic pressure to accelerate belief. Look for a move from a weaker observation to a stronger conclusion.
