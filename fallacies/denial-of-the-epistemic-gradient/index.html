Denial of the epistemic gradient

Diminishing semantic resolution by creating artificially discrete epistemic categories.

EvidentialEpistemic

Definition

Diminishing semantic resolution by creating artificially discrete epistemic categories.

Illustrative example

Either you believe in space aliens or you don't! Tell me whether you believe in space aliens!

Why it misleads

A fuller explanation of how the fallacy works and why it can look persuasive.

Evidence commonly arrives in degrees. If belief is to be warranted, it must map to the evidence. Therefore, belief is necessarily non-discrete.

Case studies

Recent or representative examples that help stress-test the pattern in practice.

Both theists and atheists often over-categorize the position of others by suggesting one must either belief or disbelieve in a god. However, unless you belief the evidence for a particular god arrives in discrete packets, it must be admitted that belief in that god will fall somewhere on a continuum.
