15 fallacies in this category.

Diagnostic prompt

If the premises were true, would the conclusion still fail to follow?

Affirmative conclusion from a negative premise

Occurs when a categorical syllogism has a positive conclusion, but at least one negative premise.

Formal

Affirming a disjunct

Concluding that one logical disjunction must be false because the other disjunct is true; A or B; A; therefore not B.

Formal

Affirming the consequent

The antecedent in a conditional is claimed to be true because the consequent is true; if A, then B; B, therefore A.

Formal

Association fallacy

Occurs when a claim is accepted or dismissed because of some irrelevant association rather than because of the merits of the claim itself.

FormalEvidentialTactical

Begging the question

Occurs when an argument quietly assumes the very point it is supposed to prove, so the conclusion is built into the premises.

Formal

Denying the antecedent

Occurs when the consequent in an indicative conditional is claimed to be false because the antecedent is false; if A, then B; not A, therefore not B.

Formal

Exclusive premises

Occurs when a categorical syllogism that is invalid because both of its premises are negative.

Formal

Existential fallacy

Argues to a conclusion that implies that a class has at least one member, but without such an implication in the premises.

Formal

Four terms fallacy

A categorical syllogism that has four terms.

Formal

Illicit major

Occurs when a categorical syllogism is invalid because its major term is undistributed in the major premise but distributed in the conclusion.

Formal

Inconsistent comparison

Occurs when complex nodes of comparison are used that make it falsely appear as if a complete comparison has been made.

FormalConceptual

Masked man fallacy

Occurs when the substitution of identical designators in a true statement leads to a potentially false one.

Formal

Negative proof fallacy

Assuming that, because a premise cannot be proven false, the premise must be true; or that, because a premise cannot be proven true, the premise must be false.

Formal

Square logic

A complex argument which loses logical cohesion somewhere in the complexity.

Formal

Undistributed middle

Occurs when the middle general term in a categorical syllogism is not distributed to more specific terms of the same category.
