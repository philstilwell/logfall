Tactical

Debate maneuvers that distract, derail, pressure, or strategically reroute the exchange.

Diagnostic prompt

Is the argument still addressing the original issue, or has the conversation been steered away?

Ad hominem

Occurs when someone treats an attack on a person's character, motives, class, or biography as if it were a refutation of that person's argument.

TacticalEmotional

Appeal to accomplishment

Occurs when an claim is deemed true based on the accomplishments of its sponsor.

EvidentialTactical

Appeal to emotion

Occurs when a conclusion is pushed mainly by triggering fear, pity, outrage, pride, or hope rather than by showing that the conclusion follows from the evidence.

EpistemicTacticalEmotional

Appeal to novelty

Occurs when something is treated as better mainly because it is new, cutting-edge, or marketed as the future.

PerspectivalTacticalEmotional

Appeal to poverty

Suggesting the truth of a conclusion necessarily negatively correlates to it's proponent's financial situation.

Tactical

Appeal to wealth

Suggesting the truth of a conclusion necessarily positively correlates to it's proponent's financial situation.

Tactical

Argument from repetition

Occurs when repetition is treated as if it adds evidence, wearing down doubt or making a claim seem true through familiarity.

Tactical

Argumentum ad baculum

Occurs when an argument is made through coercion or threats of force towards an opposing party.

TacticalEmotional

Association fallacy

Occurs when a claim is deemed true or untrue based on an association with some irrelevant element.

FormalEvidentialTactical

Bare assertion fallacy

Occurs when a contested claim is simply asserted, often confidently, without the evidence needed to justify it.

EvidentialTactical

Cherry picking

Occurs when someone selects only the evidence that supports a conclusion and ignores a wider body of evidence that weakens, qualifies, or reverses it.

EvidentialTactical

Contextomy

The selective excerpting of words from their original linguistic context in a way that distorts the intended meaning.

LinguisticTactical

Demanding negative proof

Attempting to avoid the burden of proof for some claim by demanding proof of the contrary from whoever questions that claim.

EvidentialTactical

Empty refutation

Occurs when an argument is said to be wrong, yet no information or explanation about what in the argument was wrong.

Tactical

Fallacy of many questions

Occurs when someone asks a question that presupposes something that has not been proven or accepted by all the people involved.

Tactical

False attribution

Occurs when an advocate appeals to an irrelevant, unqualified, unidentified, biased or fabricated source in support of an argument.

EvidentialTactical

False surrender

Offering an unwarranted or premature truce on a point of contention in order to misrepresent opponent's position as unprovable or inconclusive.

Tactical

If-by-whiskey

An argument that appears to support both sides of an issue by employing terms that diminish the actual incompatability between the positions.

Tactical

Judgmental language

Insulting or pejorative language to influence the recipient's judgment.

Tactical

Misleading vividness

Involves describing an occurrence, often exceptional, in vivid detail, to distort the actual severity of the problem.

Tactical

Moving the goalpost

Occurs when evidence that was supposed to satisfy a stated standard is dismissed and a new, harder standard is introduced so the conclusion never has to be reconsidered.

EvidentialTactical

Perverted analogy

Twisting an opponent's analogy to mean something broader than intended.

ConceptualTactical

Poisoning the well

Occurs when negative framing is introduced in advance so that whatever a person says next will be dismissed before it is fairly heard.

Tactical

Proof by verbosity

Submitting an argument too complex, obtuse or verbose to reasonably deal with.

LinguisticTactical

Red herring

Occurs when someone diverts attention from the unresolved issue by switching to a different issue that is easier, safer, or more emotionally useful.

TacticalEmotional

Straw man argument

Occurs when someone replaces an opponent's actual position with a weaker, more extreme, or simplified version and then refutes that easier target.

Tactical

Style over substance fallacy

Occurs when the polish, confidence, charisma, or dramatic force of a presentation is treated as if it established the quality of the argument itself.

Tactical

Thought-terminating cliché

Occurs when a commonly heard and accepted phrase is rhetorically introduced as a substitute for an actual argument.

Tactical

Tu quoque

Occurs when criticism is answered not by engaging the issue, but by pointing to similar hypocrisy or wrongdoing elsewhere.

EvidentialTactical

Two wrongs make a right

Occurs when it is assumed that, if one wrong has been committed, another wrong will remove its culpability.

EvidentialTactical

Vague insulators

Occurs when terms are intentionally chosen for their vagueness in an attempt to make a position unassailable.
