Linguistic

Confusion created by wording, ambiguity, framing, or unstable definitions.

Entries

16 fallacies in this category.

Diagnostic prompt

Has the wording shifted, blurred, or changed meaning mid-argument?

Contextomy

Occurs when words are selectively excerpted from their original context in a way that changes or distorts what the speaker meant.

LinguisticTactical

Continuum fallacy

Rejecting an argument on the grounds that one of it's concepts cannot be defined discretely into a precise category.

LinguisticConceptual

Definist fallacy

Involves the confusion between two notions by defining one in terms of the other.

LinguisticConceptual

Equivocation

Occurs when a key word or phrase slides between different meanings inside the same argument, creating the illusion of support.

Linguistic

Equivocation fallacy

In which a speaker will use a general definition of a term to denote a specific insinuation.

Linguistic

Etymological fallacy

Occurs when it is argued that the original or historical meaning of a word or phrase is necessarily similar to its actual present-day meaning.

Linguistic

Fallacy of necessity

A degree of unwarranted necessity is placed in the conclusion based on the necessity of one or more of its premises.

LinguisticConceptual

Genetic fallacy

Occurs when a claim, practice, or idea is judged mainly by its origin rather than by its present content, evidence, or merits.

LinguisticConceptual

Intentional fallacy

The assumption that the meaning intended by an author of a concept is not relevant to the understanding of that concept.

LinguisticPerspectival

No True Scotsman

Occurs when someone protects a generalization from counterexamples by redefining the group with an ad hoc 'real' or 'true' membership test.

LinguisticConceptual

Overwhelming exception

A generalization that is coupled with qualifications so restrictive that the generalization is essentially impotent.

LinguisticConceptual

Proof by verbosity

Submitting an argument too complex, obtuse or verbose to reasonably deal with.

LinguisticTactical

Reification

Occurs when an abstraction is treated as if it were a real event or physical entity.

LinguisticConceptualPerspectival

Semantic pixelization

Occurs when a term or concept with low semantic precision is chosen over an available more precise term or concept in a way that distorts the opponent's stance.

Linguistic

Suppressed correlative

An argument which tries to redefine one mutually exclusive option so that it encompasses the other option.

LinguisticConceptual

Suppressed correlative (DUPLICATE)

Occurs when a correlative is redefined so that one alternative is made impossible.
