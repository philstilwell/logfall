Appeal to probability

Occurs when someone assumes that because something could happen, it is therefore likely or inevitable that it will happen.

Base rate fallacy

Occurs when someone judges how likely a case is by focusing on vivid case-specific evidence while ignoring the underlying frequency of the thing in question.

Conjunction fallacy

The assumption that an outcome simultaneously satisfying multiple conditions is more probable than an outcome satisfying fewer of those same conditions.

Ecological fallacy

Inferences about the nature of specific individuals are based solely upon aggregate statistics collected for the group to which those individuals belong.

MathematicalConceptual

Gambler's fallacy

The incorrect belief that the likelihood of a random event can be affected by or predicted from other, independent events.

MathematicalCausal

Hasty generalization

Occurs when someone draws a broad conclusion from too little evidence, too small a sample, or a badly skewed sample.

MathematicalEvidential

Linearity fallacy

The assumption that a phenomenon functions linearly, overlooking important factors that produce non-linearity.

MathematicalConceptual

Prosecutor's fallacy

Assuming that a low probability of false matches means an improbable false match will not be found.

Mathematical

Regression fallacy

Ascribes the cause of some phenomenon to some non-statistical factor, when natural and expected statistical fluctuations may account for the apparent phenomenon.
